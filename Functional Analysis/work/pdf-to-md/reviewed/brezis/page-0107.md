$E=\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n$, so that $|E|=0$ and

$$
|f(x)|\le C_n\quad\forall n,\quad\forall x\in\Omega\setminus E;
$$

it follows that $|f(x)|\le\|f\|_\infty$ $\forall x\in\Omega\setminus E$.

**Notation.** Let $1\le p\le\infty$; we denote by $p'$ the *conjugate exponent*,

$$
\boxed{\frac1p+\frac1{p'}=1.}
$$

**• Theorem 4.6 (Hölder’s inequality).** *Assume that $f\in L^p$ and $g\in L^{p'}$ with $1\le p\le\infty$. Then $fg\in L^1$ and*

$$
\boxed{\int|fg|\le\|f\|_p\|g\|_{p'}.} \tag{1}
$$

*Proof.* The conclusion is obvious if $p=1$ or $p=\infty$; therefore we assume that $1<p<\infty$. We recall Young’s inequality:¹

$$
\boxed{ab\le\frac1p a^p+\frac1{p'}b^{p'}\quad\forall a\ge0,\quad\forall b\ge0.} \tag{2}
$$

Inequality (2) is a straightforward consequence of the concavity of the function $\log$ on $(0,\infty)$:

$$
\log\left(\frac1p a^p+\frac1{p'}b^{p'}\right)
\ge\frac1p\log a^p+\frac1{p'}\log b^{p'}=\log ab.
$$

We have

$$
|f(x)g(x)|\le\frac1p|f(x)|^p+\frac1{p'}|g(x)|^{p'}\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\Omega.
$$

It follows that $fg\in L^1$ and

$$
\int|fg|\le\frac1p\|f\|_p^p+\frac1{p'}\|g\|_{p'}^{p'}. \tag{3}
$$

Replacing $f$ by $\lambda f$ ($\lambda>0$) in (3), yields

$$
\int|fg|\le\frac{\lambda^{p-1}}p\|f\|_p^p+\frac1{\lambda p'}\|g\|_{p'}^{p'}. \tag{4}
$$

Choosing $\lambda=\|f\|_p^{-1}\|g\|_{p'}^{p'/p}$ (so as to minimize the right-hand side in (4)), we obtain (1).

---

¹ It is sometimes convenient to use the form $ab\le\varepsilon a^p+C_\varepsilon b^{p'}$ with $C_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1/(p-1)}$.
